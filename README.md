# Pocket Hunter

An incremental (idle) survivor game for Android, with iOS to follow. Your Hunter stands in the middle of the field and auto-shoots an endless horde closing in from every side, Vampire Survivors style. Enemies that reach the Hunter don't hurt them: they **stun** them and **run off with their loot**. You start in the Wandering Woods and push through **five areas**. Each has its own monsters and materials, and every area you open stays open for good. Recruit extra **Hunters** with their own specialties and station them in areas to keep earning while you hunt elsewhere.

## Core loop

| Layer | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Battlefield** | Your Hunter auto-fires at the nearest approaching enemy. Enemies arrive in packs from off-screen. One that reaches the Hunter **stuns** them and **flees**; if it gets off-screen it escapes with its gold and material. A stun always runs its course (more monsters arriving don't extend it), then a short immunity means you always get some shots off. Tap anywhere for a blast, even while stunned (your Hunter's equipped **tap ability** can make it elemental: see Hunters). Hunter abilities and items' own abilities that recharge (Reginald's potions, Glimmer's fireballs, the Wolf Spirit tome's lunge) show as **cooldown icons** along one edge: a used icon greys out and refills with colour from the top down, pulsing when it's ready; icons are as tall as the DPS meter and share its row (leaving it room), and spill into a second row (below the icons and the meter, at the top) when the row is full. An event's timer and a Guardian's health bar sit at the bottom of the battlefield by default (Settings: top or bottom), always clear of the cooldown icons and the DPS meter. **Bigger battlefields**: each area's field is 10% bigger than the Wandering Woods' per area after it (the Fey Grove 110%, the Meteor Field 200%), so the view zooms out and Hunters and monsters are drawn smaller; bigger fields hold more monsters at once. Guild Hunters holding a tome summon its creatures too (with their own lunge cooldown icon), and a stunned Guild Hunter shows the same stun bar and STUNNED! text as yours. A Hunter whose pistol, rifle or repeater is reloading shows **RELOADING!**; a staff recharging its spell shows **RECHARGING!** and a tome readying its next summon **SUMMONING!**. Each fades away from right to left as the wait runs out, its edge breaking up into pixels: grey metal and red sparks for reloads, white, gold and blue magic for recharges, and a pink, magenta and dark-blue haze for summons. |
| **Areas** | 12 areas: Wandering Woods → Fey Grove → Restless Graveyard → Forbidden Crypt → Shadowy Depths → Ember Mines → Venom Caverns → Frost Peaks → Ascendant Steps → Cloud Fortress → Meteor Field → Void Rift. The Ascendant Steps climb from the Frost Peaks into the sky, up to the Cloud Fortress and on past the sky to the space-and-meteor Meteor Field; deeper in lies the Void Rift. The Void Rift's Guardian is the **Time Eater** (30,000 Rift kills to challenge it): it only ever appears as that Guardian, with twice the HP of a normal wall, and the fight is won once its HP falls to half (a gold mark on its health bar). That plays a cutscene of the Hunters' attempt to kill it (replayable in Tutorials) and conquers the Void Rift, the end of the known world (and where Ascension will come in). Get enough kills in an area (**mastery**) to unlock its **Guardian Challenge** event (a giant version of one of the area's monsters, 45s timer; it stuns longer and bounces off). Beat it and the next area is **permanently unlocked**. **Economy**: every area's monsters are far tougher than the last (HP from 1 in the Woods to 20 quintillion in the Void Rift) and pay far more gold (1 to 5 sextillion per kill), so a few kills in a new area outpay even Empowered monsters of the old one, and gold per second climbs from about 1 to far past trillions. Each **Guardian** is a wall sized to the level you should have by then (about Lv 9 for the Woods Guardian, Lv 16 for the Grove's): expect several tries, training in between. Travel between unlocked areas from the **Areas** tab, where each area shows its mastery progress and stats: slain, gold earned, escaped, knockouts, monsters unlocked and who is stationed there. The **Areas tab** stays greyed out until the Fey Grove is unlocked (tapping it says how). It is a 3-wide grid of area icons, each with slots showing the (up to 3) Hunters stationed there. Tap an area to see its description and monsters; a bar pinned to the bottom of the menu shows its name with **Details** (a full card: monsters with their drops, monsters slain, Guardian kills, events run, gold, escapes, knockouts, Active Hunters) or **Travel**. In Details, **Active Hunters** fills a row of 3 slots (empty ones outlined) and notes when your own Hunter is there; tap any slot to open the assign screen, where you drag Hunters into or out of the area's slots, double-tap one to assign it to a free slot, or tap one to get an **Assign** / **Unassign** button right on its icon; **All Areas** there opens the all-areas Hunter view. Area tiles also take gestures: **double-tap** one to open its Details, **hold** one to travel there (a gold bar fills while you hold). The **all-areas Hunter view** (also opened from a Hunter's location button, and from **Hunters** in the full-screen Areas tab) shows every area with its slots and every recruited Hunter as an icon along the bottom (marked with where they are, or 💤). Tap or drag a Hunter to show their name with **Details** and **Assign** (then tap an area) or **Unassign**; drag one onto an area with room to station them, onto a Hunter in a full area to swap the two (a line-up of who leaves, an arrow and who comes in hovers above the slot), or onto the bottom tray to rest them. **Full-screen menu**: long-press any tab, or swipe the tab bar up, to raise the menu over the battlefield (the fight carries on underneath); the top bar stays, reading **Current Area** and its name. Tap the red strip above the tabs, swipe the tab bar down, long-press a tab again or press back to drop it. While raised, the Areas tab gets a header with the selected area's name and a **Hunters** button (in place of the area name by Details and Travel) that opens the all-areas Hunter view. |
| **Monsters & archetypes** | 77 monsters: 5 to 7 in each area (11 in the Void Rift). The first comes with the area and the rest are unlocked in the Bestiary. Each has its own **HP, speed, spawn rate, pack size, gold**, **material** it drops, weaknesses and resistances, and an **archetype**: Slime, Beast, Undead, Demon, Elemental, Humanoid, Plant or Dragon. Wandering Woods: Green Slime (Green Slime), Twiggling (Twigs), Forest Wolf (Wolf Pelt + Beast Bone), Goblin (Scrap), Brown Bear (Bear Claw + Beast Bone). Fey Grove: Toadstool (Mushroom Cap), Pixie (Pixie Dust), Unicorn (Enchanted Bone + Unicorn Horn), Walking Man Trap (Enchanted Thorn), Treant (Sentient Sap), **Stone Golem** (Awoken Rock; rarer Obsidian and Iron Ore). Graveyard: Skeleton (Undead Bone), Zombie (Rotting Flesh, and sometimes gear), Vampire Bat (Vampire Essence + Bat Wing), Cultist (Sacred Text + Robe Piece), Wraith (Nightmare Wisp). Some monsters drop **two materials**: the second comes at half the drop chance. **Golems** (Constructs) drop several, each rarer than the last and rolling on its own; the Bestiary tags the rarer ones Uncommon, Rare, Very Rare or Legendary. Their metals and gems pay for Refine and Enchant. Besides the Stone, Ore and Geode Golems: the **Venomite Golem** (Venom Caverns: Cobalt Ore, rarer Venomite and Mithril Ore), the **Glacial Golem** (Frost Peaks: Frost Iron, rarer Coldsteel) and **Crystal Golem** (Frost Peaks: Aquamarine, rarer Sapphire), the **Sky Golem** (Ascendant Steps: Skysteel, rarer Adamantite), the **Storm Golem** (Cloud Fortress: Opal, rarer Stormstone), the **Meteor Golem** (Meteor Field: Star Iron, rarer Orichalcum) and the **Void Golem** (Void Rift: Voidsteel, rarer Void Prism). Crypt: Carrion Crow (Undead Feather), Possessed Puppet (Possessed String), Bone Knight (Enchanted Bone, and sometimes gear), Possessed Armor (Enchanted Scrap, and sometimes gear), Mummy (Possessed Cloth), Necromancer (Forbidden Text); the Puppet and the Armor are **Constructs**, a new archetype with its own evolution tree. Shadowy Depths: Gloomcrawler (Dark Claw), Duskmoth (Gloom Silk), Shadow Haunt (Shadow Wisp), Gazing Eye (Umbral Eyeball), Umbral Ooze (Umbral Slime), Abyssal Lurker (Umbral Pearl). Ember Mines: Imp (Demon Bone + Demon Wing), Magma Slime (Magma Slime), Fire Beetle (Chitin), Salamander (Ember), Hellhound (Demon Tooth + Demon Bone), **Ore Golem** (Iron Ore; rarer Silver Ore and Gold Ore), **Geode Golem** (Quartz; rarer Amethyst, Topaz, Emerald and Diamond). Venom Caverns: Kobold, Basilisk, Toxic Bloat, Broodmother, Cave Troll. Peaks: Ice Wolf, Frost Golem, Snow Wraith, Yeti, Frost Sprite. Ascendant Steps: Harpy, Snow Owl, Griffin, Ice Wyvern, Frost Giant. Cloud Fortress: Cloudling, Thunderbird, Sky Knight, Valkyrie, Storm Titan (drop Storm Feathers and Thunder Crystals). Meteor Field: Meteorite, Comet Wisp, Rock Mite, Astral Sentinel, Star Eater (drop Meteor Iron and Stardust). Rift: Shadow Slime, Void Horror, Lich, Shade, Watcher, Dark Knight, Succubus, Chimera, Void Wyrm, Behemoth. **Guardians** are boss versions of an area's monsters, met only in its Guardian Challenge, each dropping its own material (10 per kill): the **King of Slimes** (Royal Slime), **Pixie Queen** (Regal Essence), **Skeleton King** (Calcium Crystals), **Awoken Lich** (Eldritch Texts), **Beholden Watcher** (Everwatching Eyes) and **Demon Lord** (Forsaken Souls) for the first six areas; later areas still use one of their own monsters for now, and the Void Rift has the Time Eater. Guardian materials go into each area's prized recipes (e.g. the Slime Vial needs Royal Slime, the Flame Brand Forsaken Souls). New areas reuse the materials of the area before them. |
| **Bestiary** (gold) | Each area starts with one monster; **unlock** the others to grow its horde. **Training** opens once you've slain 20 slimes (the Train button shows the count until then) and **Empower** once you've slain 100 slimes (a one-time popup explains the Bestiary). Empower a monster with gold (the ×1/×10/×100/MAX switch, pinned to the top of the Bestiary, sets how many sessions): each session adds +4.2% HP, +20% gold, +2.1% material drops and +4.5% spawns (so the horde keeps up with your damage, and more monsters mean more gold); every 5 sessions is a level (and an evolution point), up to **Lv 30**, where its evolution tree is exactly complete (about ×7 HP, ×30 gold, ×7.5 spawns). The first sessions are cheap and each costs 12.5% more than the last, so a monster reaches Lv 30 about two areas later (Green Slimes around the end of the Restless Graveyard, or soon after reaching the Forbidden Crypt). Each card shows what the monster drops, its level and progress toward the next. Tap a monster (cards say **Expand ›**) for its full view, with two sub-tabs: **Stats** (what it drops, Empower, its stats including how many you've slain) and **Evolution** (its tree; the tab shows unspent points). Each row of the tree opens after slaying enough of that monster (100 for the root, 1,000 for the branches, 5,000 for the signature nodes, 20,000 for the capstone; rarer monsters need fewer), so you can't jump ahead. Each archetype has its own tree, shaped like a Hunter's: a signature root (Slimes: +25% HP and +25% gold), three branches, **Wealth** (gold), **Horde** (spawns) and **Harvest** (materials), a signature node under each (Slimes' **Small Hordes** makes them arrive in bigger packs; Beasts', Demons' and Dragons' gold nodes also make them faster), and a capstone that costs whatever points are left (29 in all, so every tree completes at Lv 30). **Every monster has its own tree** (its archetype's eight nodes, so evolving Green Slimes doesn't evolve Magma Slimes), and its capstone is its **Legacy**: besides its own bonus, every monster of its archetype, wherever it's hunted, gains a small chance to drop a material (a share of its drop chance). A monster whose boss version is its area's Guardian passes on the Guardian's material at 10% (Green Slime: Royal Slime to every slime; Pixie: Regal Essence; Skeleton: Calcium Crystal; Mummy: Eldritch Text; Gazing Eye: Everwatching Eye; Imp: Forsaken Soul); the others pass on their rarest drop at half its share (Geode Golem: Diamonds to every Construct), or their only drop at 25% (Twiggling: Twigs to every Plant). Legacies stack, and the monster's Stats show the **Legacy drops** its archetype has. Saves from before Lv 30 refund their evolution points. Tapping a node shows the **Points required** and **Points available** under its name and a big **Evolve** button. Permanent, and it applies wherever the monster is hunted, including by stationed Hunters. Older saves turn each Swarm and Bounty level into an Empower session. |
| **Hunters** (gold) | Every Hunter, you included, **trains** with gold: each session adds a little damage and costs a bit more than the last. Enough sessions raise their **level** (3 sessions for Lv 2, then 4 more for Lv 3, and so on), and every level earns a **skill point** for their **skill tree**. Every tree branches from a signature root node into Attack Power, Attack Speed and Recovery Speed, then a signature node under each branch and a capstone at the bottom; a node needs a point in the one it hangs from. Some nodes cost more than one point (shown on the node), so every Guild Hunter's first tree costs exactly 39 points and is complete at **Lv 40**. Then a gold **Ascend** node appears (10 points, so **Lv 50**); Guild Hunters can't train past Lv 50 until they ascend. Their training cost grows 8.5% per session up to Lv 30, then 3% per session (a little faster than the damage each session adds), so gold paces the whole climb to Ascension. Ascending gives them a new title (e.g. Reginald the Alchemist becomes the Archalchemist), keeps Lv 50, starts the level curve over (1 session to Lv 51, 2 to Lv 52, ...) at the same training price, opens an **Ascended tree** (50 points: Awakening, Mastery, Fervor, Resolve, Precision, Reach, Fortune, and a capstone of their own that opens at Lv 100) and raises the cap to **Lv 100**. **Your Hunter** ("the Hunter") has three **ability nodes** between their signature nodes and their capstone (Apex Hunter, which needs any one of them): **Flame Burst**, **Thunderclap** and **Frost Nova** each unlock a **tap ability**, equipped in the **Abilities** tab (one at a time; or the plain **Blast** in your weapon's type). With one equipped, a tap blast deals that element and always sets off its effect on everything it hits: Flame Burst sets them burning, Thunderclap arcs a bolt of lightning from each to another monster nearby, Frost Nova chills them for 3s. Their first tree goes on below the capstone (Hunter's Might, Hunter's Haste, Iron Will, Deadly Taps, Keen Eye, Wide Taps and **Legend**; 89 points, done at Lv 90), then their own gold **Ascend** node (10 points) at **Lv 100**, which makes them **the Slayer**: the level curve starts over and the **Slayer's tree** (100 points: Slayer's Oath, Carnage, Frenzy, Unbreakable, Precision, Tap Fury, Tap Reach, and **Godslayer** at Lv 200) opens up, with training to **Lv 200**. Once a Hunter has ascended, a switch at the top of their Skills tab flips between the new tree and their first one. Their training cost grows 7.5% per session up to Lv 30, then 3% per session like the Guild Hunters', so each richer area pays for the next stretch of levels all the way to Lv 200. Lance's root, **Zone of Protection**, is his shield, and his **Rallying Oath** gives every other Hunter a hit. The **Hunter Guild** has 13 recruitable Hunters (a locked Hunter's card tells a short story of where they are and how to win them over), each with their own **attack style**, range and perk (table below). Each card shows the Hunter's art, a gold dot in the corner counting unspent skill points, their name, ability, location and DPS, equipped gear and a **Train** button (the ×1/×10/×100/MAX switch, pinned to the top of the Hunters tab and repeated in each Hunter's Overview, sets how many sessions). **Tap a card** (they say **Expand ›** in the corner) for a full-screen view with two sub-tabs (three for your Hunter): **Overview** (training, then **Equipment**: each slot, tap one to choose from your unequipped gear that fits; then DPS, damage, attack rate, range, stun time, shield, crit, **enemies slain**, stationing), **Skills** (the tree; tapping a node shows the **Points required** and **Points available** under its name and a big **Acquire** button) and, for your Hunter, **Abilities** (tap abilities); close it with the floating **✕** at the bottom (on the right, or the left when left-handed) to return to the battlefield. The location under their name is a button that opens the all-areas Hunter view. **Station** up to 3 Hunters per area (besides yours); Hunters sharing an area farm it together. They earn gold and materials there at 80% efficiency (offline too). Visit their area and they fight beside you. Monsters head for the **nearest** Hunter, so stationed Hunters can be stunned too. |
| **Inventory** (materials) | Three sub-tabs: **Equipment** (the gear and Upgrades you've crafted; tap one for details. **Filter** narrows it by Type (cycle through Melee, Ranged, Magic, Armor, Accessories and Upgrades), Rarity, Damage type and Stars), **Crafting** (every recipe you know and the Upgrades, as 5-wide grids of icons (greyed out when you can't afford them), with the Upgrades split into Available (not crafted yet, or with stars still to earn) and Completed (5★); tap one for its card over the Battle Field: details, the recipe with what you hold, how many you own, and a Craft button that pops up "Crafted <item>!") and **Materials** (what you've collected from slain monsters; a one-time popup points you to Crafting the first time you can afford a recipe; a drop chance past 100% drops several, since every full 100% is a guaranteed drop and the rest is the chance of one more, e.g. 350% = 3 + a 50% chance; tap one for its card: a description, how many you have and have gained in total, and every monster that drops it; tap a monster to open its Bestiary entry). Craft gear into your inventory, upgrade it from 1★ up to 5★ (5★ gear has 10× its 1★ stats; each star costs the old levels it replaces) or salvage it for half its materials (not while someone's wearing it: unequip it first); a piece with modifiers also returns half of what each modifier it holds now cost, materials and gold (a modifier you replaced is gone, and so is what it cost). Upgrading costs materials **and gold**: 300 kills' worth of gold in the piece's area for 1★ → 2★, doubling each star. Upgrades also go 1★ to 5★ (1★ is their old Lv 1 and 5★ their old maximum, with Common and Uncommon ones capped at the old Lv 15 since rarer gear and Upgrades take over from there). Upgrades have rarities like gear (Whetstone and Quickdraw Gloves are Common, up to the Artifact Void Engine). Rarer Upgrades pick up where Common ones stop: the Rare **Grave Whetstone** (Bone, Rotten Flesh) and **Batwing Gloves** (Bat Wing, Bone) add damage and attack rate on top of the Whetstone and Gloves. Most boost every Hunter wherever they are; **area Upgrades** change one area instead, like the **Forest Idol** (Uncommon): Wandering Woods monsters have ×2 HP and ×2 gold (up to ×3 gold at 5★), so an area you've outgrown pays better. **Upgrades**: the 10 original permanent items that boost every Hunter (Whetstone, Quickdraw Gloves, Monster Lure, Scavenger's Pouch, Bone Mail, Split Bow, Golden Idol, Frost Lance, Soul Lantern, Void Engine). Recipes use materials from each area in turn, so every new area opens up new gear. **Weapon classes**: every weapon has one, and it shapes how it attacks. Your Hunter's weapon slot takes any weapon and attacks the way it does: a **Dagger** stabs in a quick burst of 3 at one monster (thrown when it's further off; some daggers stab more but attack slower: the Umbral Dagger 5), and the last stab knocks it back; a **Sword** sweeps through every monster in front, a **Glaive** cleaves wider and heavier; a **Spear** thrusts straight out in a long, wide band that hits everything in it; a **Hammer** is very slow but crushing: it **slams the ground**, hitting every monster close around the Hunter (behind too; area size and Size both make the ring bigger) and sending them flying; every melee hit knocks a surviving monster back (Guardians hold their ground); a **Shortbow** is the basic bow, firing quick, light shots nonstop; a **Longbow** fires slow, heavy, long shots, and each arrow carries on a short way (further with more range) through its target into whatever is just behind; a **Crossbow** always pierces at least one more monster; guns hit harder than their bow counterparts but fire a magazine, then reload: a **Pistol** (close range, 6 shots), a **Rifle** (very long range, piercing, 4 shots, long reload) and a **Repeater** (sprays volleys of 3 bolts at random angles within a fan; each extra bolt of a volley that hits the same monster deals 50% more, so ×1.5 then ×2; 6 volleys per magazine); magic weapons have short cooldowns, unlike melee: a **Wand** is the basic one (quick, light bolts at shorter range); a **Scepter** fires slower, longer-range bolts that bounce on to 2 more monsters nearby; a **Staff** casts 5 bolts (some staffs more or fewer: the Sky Staff 3, the Soulfire Staff 8), then a big spell that blasts everything around its target, then a short cooldown; a **Focus** bursts with power whenever monsters come close, hitting every monster around the Hunter (× area size), or, on some focuses (the Ember Focus), sends out a **wave** that expands twice as far, hitting each monster as it passes; a **Tome** summons a spirit that hunts monsters across the field for 8 seconds before fading, with a cooldown before the next (the first is the Rare Tome of Wisps). Some items have abilities of their own: the Uncommon **Tome of the Wolf Spirit** (Wolf Pelts) summons wolves that lunge at a monster every 4s, and a lunge that lands bites for ×2.5. Guild Hunters keep their own signature attack, and their weapon's class reshapes its speed, weight and range. Each class's sustained speed × damage (guns counting their reloads) is about the same, so none is simply better. |
| **Loot** | Some monsters carry **gear**: Zombies, Bone Knights, Possessed Armor, Imps, Kobolds, Cave Trolls, Yetis, Frost Giants, Sky Knights, Valkyries, Astral Sentinels, Dark Knights and Liches (about 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 6,000 kills), and every **Guardian** drops a piece when it falls. Loot is a random piece from the area's own gear (60%) or an older area's (40%), at 1★ (sometimes 2★), and a popup says what dropped. Stationed Hunters loot too, online and offline (the Welcome Back screen counts it). **Auto Salvage** (a button beside **Filter** in Inventory → Equipment, from the Restless Graveyard on; it's lit while any rarity is set): its menu lets you tap the rarities to salvage, and looted gear of those rarities turns straight into half its materials as it drops; **Salvage now** clears out the unequipped pieces of those rarities you already have (equipped gear is kept). |
| **Equipment** | Every Hunter has 3 slots, always including **Armor**. A new game starts your Hunter with a **Short Sword** and **Common Garb** (no bonus) on and a **Short Bow** in the inventory; starting gear can't be crafted, but it can drop as loot. **Gear tiers come from the recipe**: gear made from materials found further into an area is a little stronger (in the Wandering Woods, Green Slime gear is tier 1.0, Twig gear 1.2, Wolf Pelt and Beast Bone gear 1.3, Scrap 1.5, Bear Claw 1.6 and Royal Slime gear 1.9; weapon damage grows smoothly toward the next area's). **Wandering Woods gear** — Common: Slime Sword (sword), Forest Bow (shortbow), Fur Coat (light armor), Bone Armor (heavy armor), Bone Spear (spear), Longbow, Glaive; Uncommon: Beast Blade (dagger), Forest Blade (sword), Nature Bow (shortbow), Crossbow, Shield, Royal Glaive, Leather Armor (light), Plate Armor (heavy), Slime Wand (wand, Poison), **Spark Wand** (wand, Fire). Of the Woods's blades only the Beast Blade makes monsters bleed. Accessory bonuses grow with stars like other stats. Empty slots show a dashed outline. **Damage starts from the weapon**: every hit begins at the weapon's own base damage (the Short Sword and Short Bow: 1, the same as a green slime's HP; bare hands: 1), set by its rarity and made heavier or lighter by its type (a hammer's base is higher than a dagger's). Upgrading the weapon raises that base (×1.3 at 2★ up to ×2.6 at 5★), and everything else multiplies it: training, skills, Upgrades, and the % damage on armor and accessories. Your Hunter starts with no bonuses at all. **Armor** comes in four kinds, each trading attack speed, damage and **shield charges** (hits you can take before being stunned; they recharge one every 4s): **Light armor** (attack speed, a little damage, shorter stuns; no penalty), **Heavy armor** (shield charges and shorter stuns, but a fixed −10% attack rate), **Robes** (damage and bigger area effects, no shield) and **Shields** (the most shield charges, but a fixed −8% damage). Armor stats grow with the area it comes from and with stars (shield charges grow slowly: 5★ has twice the 1★ charges); penalties never grow and show in red. From Very Rare up, armor has a **special**: **Thorns** (monsters that reach you take damage: Chitin Carapace, Void Plate), **Shield burst** (each block blasts everything nearby: Drake Shield fire, Frost Mail frost, Stormplate lightning, Star Aegis radiant), **Evasion** (a chance to slip a monster entirely, no stun and no shield used: Griffin Hide 25%, Starweave Jerkin 35%) and **Pulse** (a burst around you every 5s: Ember Robe fire, Cloudsilk Robe lightning, Soul Vestments void). **Accessories** can have abilities found on nothing else: the **Puppeteer's Doll** (Rare, crafted from the Crypt's Puppet String and Mummy Wrap) summons 2 puppets every 8s that hunt for 6s like a tome's creatures; the **Flame Brand** gives melee attacks a 30% chance to send out a wave of fire; the **Thunder Totem** calls lightning down on 3 monsters every 3s; the **Frostbite Charm** chills every monster near you. **Summon types**: every summoned creature has a type, shown on its card: the Tome of Wisps summons Spirits, the Tome of the Wolf Spirit Beasts, the Necronomicon Undead, the Tome of Storms Elementals, and the Puppeteer's Doll Constructs. Some Hunters command a type better, whatever summoned it: Deku's Beasts and Theon's Constructs hit 50% harder. A Hunter whose special is a summon (Deku's wolves, Theon's puppets) sends creatures that copy their **weapon**: its damage type and its chance to inflict that type's status effect (Theon with a Spark Wand fields fire-biting puppets that set monsters burning); with no weapon they're Physical. **Gear abilities have their own base damage** (shown on the card, rising with stars like a weapon's), multiplied by the wearer's bonuses (training, skills, Upgrades, % damage) but never by their weapon's damage; recharging ones get a cooldown icon. Most (and you) have Ranged weapon · Armor · Accessory. **Lance**: Melee · Armor · Accessory. **Wilhelm**: Long-range weapon · Short-range weapon (used when monsters get close) · Armor. **Reginald**: Weapon (ranged **or** magic) · Armor · Accessory. **Glimmer**: Magic weapon · Robe (robes only) · Accessory. Tap a slot in the Equipment section of a Hunter's Overview to open the gear picker: the equipped piece's card on top, the piece you're inspecting below it (showing what each stat gains or loses) with an Equip button to swap, and your whole inventory as a grid; a piece can only be worn by one Hunter at a time. **Painted backgrounds**: the Wandering Woods battlefield is a painted forest clearing built from CraftPix's grassland tileset (`public/backgrounds/forest/`, `src/render/backgrounds.ts`): textured grass, flowers, tufts and pebbles everywhere, bushes, stones and stumps outside the middle, and fruit trees, boulders and vine-covered ruins around the edges, so the centre stays clear. The **Fey Grove** uses CraftPix's forest tileset the same way (`public/backgrounds/glade/`), its greens shifted to fey pinks and purples (trunks, stones, mushrooms and ruins keep their colours) on lavender grass: reeds and mushrooms throughout, pink and violet bushes and trees, and overgrown statues and ruins at the edges. Bleeding is the only effect that stacks, up to 8 bleeds at once; every other effect is one instance per monster, and a new one refreshes it (keeping the stronger; a monster has at most one acid puddle). **Void** opens a **gravity well** on the monster it hits: for 1.5s, monsters within reach are pulled in toward it until they touch it, bunching them up for area attacks (Guardians and Void-resistant monsters hold their ground). ## Tech

- **TypeScript + HTML5 Canvas**, bundled with **Vite**. No game engine and no image assets: everything is drawn with simple shapes.
- **Look**: Game Boy Advance-style. A bundled pixel font (Pixelify Sans, so it works offline), square windows with thick borders and hard shadows, and a battlefield drawn at one canvas pixel per screen point, then scaled up crisply. Each area tints the menus with a muted (desaturated) version of its palette over a darker backdrop, so the brightly coloured battlefield stands out, and gives the battlefield its own ground colour (`palette` and `ground` in `AREAS`; applied by `src/ui/theme.ts`).
- **Capacitor** wraps the web build as a native Android app (`android/`). The same code becomes the iOS app later.
- **Snappy menus in big saves**: menus only build what's on screen. The Equipment list draws 48 tiles at a time (more as you scroll), the Bestiary builds the current area's monsters first and each other area's as you scroll near it, Inventory builds only its open sub-tab, and off-screen cards skip layout (`content-visibility`). Refreshes rewrite text only when it changed, farming rates and DPS shown on cards are recomputed at most once a second (or right after a tap), monster portraits are measured together then drawn, and gear lookups by id use an index instead of scanning the inventory.
- Saves use `@capacitor/preferences` (SharedPreferences / UserDefaults natively, localStorage on web). It autosaves every 10s and when the app is backgrounded.

```
src/
  core/        pure game logic, no DOM (unit-tested)
    balance.ts   every tunable number and content table (areas, monsters, archetypes, Hunters, items, hangar)
    game.ts      economy & progression: areas, events & Guardians, Hunters & stationing, bestiary, shops, forge, background farming
    field.ts     the battlefield simulation: spawning, movement, attack styles, ranges, stuns & shields, fleeing, Guardians
    offline.ts   offline cap + ticket regen
    state.ts     save format + migration
    save.ts      persistence
  render/      canvas: battlefield view, particles, sprite loader
  assets/sprites/  drop-in artwork (optional)
  ui/          DOM panels (Hunters / Areas / Bestiary / Equipment / Events), modals
tests/         vitest: economy, field, events, saves, and a bot that plays the real battlefield to check pacing
```

## Running it

```bash
npm install
npm run dev        # play in a browser at http://localhost:5173 (use phone view in devtools)

```

Balancing: `SIM_SWEEP=1 npx vitest run tests/progression.test.ts --silent=false` runs a bot on the real (headless) battlefield and prints when each area unlocks, both for a **typical player** and for nonstop play. The typical player has a 40-minute first session, then checks in at 08:00 (15 min), 13:00 (10 min) and 18:00 (20 min) daily, with offline gains in between. Current pacing for that player: **Fey Grove ~day 0.6**, **Graveyard ~day 2.6**, **Crypt ~day 5.6**, **Depths ~day 10.5**, **Caves ~day 18**, **Venom Caverns ~day 23.5**, **Frost Peaks ~day 32**, **Ascendant Steps ~day 41.5**, **Cloud Fortress ~day 48.5**, **Meteor Field ~day 58.5**, **Void Rift ~day 72**, and the **Time Eater** around **day 90** (the Rift and Time Eater estimated from kill rates). Every Guardian through the Meteor Field falls in one to three tries, so each area's mastery is the clock. Each area's monsters reach Lv 30 about two areas later (Green Slimes during the Crypt). Gold runs about 1/s in the Woods, 100/s in the Grove, 100K/s in the Graveyard, 10M/s in the Crypt, 100B/s in the Depths, 100T/s in the Mines and Caverns, and sextillions per second in the Rift. `SIM_OUT=pace.json` writes the typical player's unlock times in hours. The pacing test (run by CI after the APK is built, without blocking it) flags drift out of range. `CALIBRATE=1 npx vitest run tests/calibrate.test.ts --silent=false` compares the offline/background model against the live battlefield for several Hunter strengths. The gates are each area's Guardian HP and mastery kills (`AREAS` in `balance.ts`).

### On an Android phone

**Easiest:** every push to GitHub runs the **Build** workflow, which uploads `pocket-hunter-debug-apk`. Download it from the workflow run's *Artifacts*, copy the APK to your phone, and install it. You'll need to allow "install unknown apps".

**With Android Studio:**

```bash
npm run android    # builds, syncs into android/, opens Android Studio → press Run
```

### iOS (later, needs a Mac with Xcode)

```bash
npm install
npx cap add ios
npm run ios        # opens Xcode → pick a device → Run
```

## Roadmap ideas

- More enchantments (spells, on-crit and on-hit effects) and crafted Enchantments that replace an existing one
- Sound effects & haptics (`@capacitor/haptics`) on hits, crits and kills
- Local notification when tickets are full or the offline cap is reached (`@capacitor/local-notifications`)
- Active skills with cooldowns (nova, orbiting blades, freeze) and more weapon types
- Enemy traits (splitters, shielded, ranged), elite variants, Guardian attack patterns
- A prestige layer that fits permanent areas (e.g. harder "tiers" of each area for bigger rewards)
- More Hunters, Hunter gear slots, and more areas
- More area events (each area's own twist on the horde)
- Boss-only rare materials and legendary items; achievements
- Rewarded ads ("double your offline gold") or IAP; cloud save
- Replace procedural art with a commissioned sprite set
- Release signing & Play Store listing (`./gradlew bundleRelease`)
