# Pocket Hunter

An incremental (idle) survivor game for Android, with iOS to follow. Your Hunter stands in the middle of the field and auto-shoots an endless horde closing in from every side, Vampire Survivors style. Enemies that reach the Hunter don't hurt them: they **stun** them and **run off with their loot**. You start in the Wandering Woods and push through **five areas**. Each has its own monsters and materials, and every area you open stays open for good. Recruit extra **Hunters** with their own specialties and station them in areas to keep earning while you hunt elsewhere.

## Core loop

| Layer | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Battlefield** | Monsters walk around rocks, stones and tree stumps (anything from about 24 pixels up) as well as trees, but over pebbles, flowers and grass. Each monster is drawn at a random size from 0.5× to 1.5× (mostly near 1×; its hit size is unchanged), and 1 in 10,000 is a **gold monster** (gold, glinting) that pays 100× the gold and has 100× the chance to drop materials. Guardians wear the Events tab's pixel crown. A dazed Hunter wobbles, weapon and all, over a framed stun bar (no stars); shield charges show as little shields under them, centred and spreading out from the middle. Summons wear a monster's art (wolf, puppet, golem, or a wraith, skeleton or sprite by their kind) tinted toward their element, or with a purple outline when they're Physical. Your Hunter auto-fires at the nearest approaching enemy. Enemies arrive in packs from off-screen. One that reaches the Hunter **stuns** them and **flees**; if it gets off-screen it escapes with its gold and material. A stun always runs its course (more monsters arriving don't extend it), then a short immunity means you always get some shots off. Tap anywhere for a blast, even while stunned (your Hunter's equipped **tap ability** can make it elemental: see Hunters). Hunter abilities and items' own abilities that recharge (Andrejz's potions, Glimmer's fireballs, the Wolf Spirit tome's lunge) show as **cooldown icons** along one edge: a used icon greys out and refills with colour from the top down, pulsing when it's ready; icons are as tall as the DPS meter and share its row (leaving it room), and spill into a second row (below the icons and the meter, at the top) when the row is full. An event's timer and a Guardian's health bar (framed silver-edged bars: an event's timer takes the colour of the monster it's about, green for the Slime Swarm; a Guardian gets a thick health bar in its own colour over a thinner light-orange timer; the labels above have a black outline) sit at the bottom of the battlefield by default (Settings: top or bottom), always clear of the cooldown icons and the DPS meter. **Pick-up pop-ups**: materials a kill drops pop up in a corner of the battlefield (bottom left by default; Settings: on or off, and which corner), a line per material with its icon and how many came in, outlined in black with no box behind them, the newest nearest the corner; more of the same while its row is up adds to the count and keeps it up another 2 seconds. At most four rows show at once, and the golems' **ores and gems** (and their stones) push out the common drops' rows (a common drop never pushes out an ore's or gem's). Only kills on the field pop up, so you see the area you're in, not stationed Hunters' farming; the lines keep clear of the DPS meter and the cooldown icons. During a rush event with its bar on their edge they sit past the bar, so you can watch the rush's haul; during a Guardian fight they're hidden (and nothing piles up), as the fight matters more than its drops; the Guardian's own drop pops up once it falls. **Area effects grow with your Hunters**: every area-of-effect size (Hunter specials like Andrejz's puddles, Glimmer's fireballs, Sciirj's punch, Gideon's spikes, Themus's wave, Ted's dice blasts and Thomas's copies; staff fireballs; slams and waves; gear bursts like Thorns, Shield Burst and Fireburst; chill auras) grows 3% per level of the Hunter casting it (×1.75 at Lv 26, ×2.5 at Lv 51, ×7 for a Lv 200 Slayer), wherever they fight: a typical player's levels keep pace with the later areas' wider views, and a powered-up Hunter sent back to an old area can cover most of the screen. Area-size modifiers add on top and are worth chasing: the **Expanse** refine rolls +15–30% (accessories, robes, hammers and spell weapons) and the **Amplify** enchantment +20–40% (any piece). **Floating text** (damage numbers, STUNNED!, BLOCK…) scales with the characters, so it shrinks as later areas zoom out (never below 7px on screen, to stay legible), and damage numbers are a quarter smaller than they were (crits 12, weak-spot crits 14). **Characters** (Hunters, monsters, wolves and puppets) are drawn 1.67× their world size, so the Woods' Hunters look as they would at a zoom of 1 while the view, ranges and hit areas stay the same; later areas zoom out from there. **Bigger battlefields**: each area's field is 10% bigger than the Wandering Woods' per area after it (the Fey Grove 110%, the Meteor Field 200%), so the view zooms out and Hunters and monsters are drawn smaller; bigger fields hold more monsters at once. From the Shadowy Depths on, the view jumps out a big step further (`DEEP_ZOOM_OUT`: 60% more of the Woods' view on top of the area's size, so the Depths shows about 1.5× the Crypt's width), so the Hunters are much smaller, with wide open ground round them for monsters that keep their distance. Guild Hunters holding a tome summon its creatures too (with their own lunge cooldown icon), and a stunned Guild Hunter shows the same stun bar and STUNNED! text as yours. A Hunter whose pistol, rifle or repeater is reloading shows **RELOADING!**; a staff recharging its spell shows **RECHARGING!** and a tome readying its next summon **SUMMONING!**. Each fades away from right to left as the wait runs out, its edge breaking up into pixels: grey metal and red sparks for reloads, white, gold and blue magic for recharges, and a pink, magenta and dark-blue haze for summons. |
| **Areas** | 12 areas: Wandering Woods → Fey Grove → Restless Graveyard → Forbidden Crypt → Shadowy Depths → Ember Mines → Venom Caverns → Frost Peaks → Ascendant Steps → Cloud Fortress → Meteor Field → Void Rift. The Ascendant Steps climb from the Frost Peaks into the sky, up to the Cloud Fortress and on past the sky to the space-and-meteor Meteor Field; deeper in lies the Void Rift. The Void Rift's Guardian is the **Time Eater** (30,000 Rift kills to challenge it): it only ever appears as that Guardian, with twice the HP of a normal wall, and the fight is won once its HP falls to half (a gold mark on its health bar). That plays a cutscene of the Hunters' attempt to kill it (replayable in Tutorials) and conquers the Void Rift, the end of the known world (and where Ascension will come in). Get enough kills in an area (**mastery**) to unlock its **Guardian Challenge** event (a giant version of one of the area's monsters, 45s timer; it stuns longer and bounces off). Beat it and the next area is **permanently unlocked**. **Economy**: every area's monsters are far tougher than the last (HP from 1 in the Woods to 20 quintillion in the Void Rift) and pay far more gold (1 to 5 sextillion per kill), so a few kills in a new area outpay even Empowered monsters of the old one, and gold per second climbs from about 1 to far past trillions. Each **Guardian** is a wall sized to the level you should have by then (about Lv 9 for the Woods Guardian, Lv 16 for the Grove's): expect several tries, training in between. Travel between unlocked areas from the **Areas** tab, where each area shows its mastery progress and stats: slain, gold earned, escaped, knockouts, monsters unlocked and who is stationed there. The **Areas tab** stays greyed out until the Fey Grove is unlocked (tapping it says how). It is a 3-wide grid of area icons, each with slots showing the (up to 3) Hunters stationed there. Tap an area to see its description and monsters; a bar pinned to the bottom of the menu shows its name with **Details** (a full card: monsters with their drops, monsters slain, Guardian kills, events run, gold, escapes, knockouts, Active Hunters) or **Travel**. In Details, **Active Hunters** fills a row of 3 slots (empty ones outlined) and notes when your own Hunter is there; tap any slot to open the assign screen, where you drag Hunters into or out of the area's slots, double-tap one to assign it to a free slot, or tap one to get an **Assign** / **Unassign** button right on its icon; **All Areas** there opens the all-areas Hunter view. **Observe** (in Details, for another area with Hunters stationed there) shows that area's battlefield with just those Hunters fighting on their own, run on a copy of the save so you can judge how they cope without your Hunter travelling: a bar over the battlefield names the area and who's there, and **Back to my Hunter** returns to your own fight (which carried on unseen; their real gains go on as usual). Area tiles also take gestures: **double-tap** one to open its Details, **hold** one to travel there (a gold bar fills while you hold). The **all-areas Hunter view** (also opened from a Hunter's location button, and from **Hunters** in the full-screen Areas tab) shows every area with its slots and every recruited Hunter as an icon along the bottom (marked with where they are, or 💤). Tap or drag a Hunter to show their name with **Details** and **Assign** (then tap an area) or **Unassign**; drag one onto an area with room to station them, onto a Hunter in a full area to swap the two (a line-up of who leaves, an arrow and who comes in hovers above the slot), or onto the bottom tray to rest them. **Full-screen menu**: long-press any tab, or swipe the tab bar up, to raise the menu over the battlefield (the fight carries on underneath); the top bar stays, reading **Current Area** and its name. Tap the red strip above the tabs, swipe the tab bar down, long-press a tab again or press back to drop it. While raised, the Areas tab gets a header with the selected area's name and a **Hunters** button (in place of the area name by Details and Travel) that opens the all-areas Hunter view. |
| **Monsters & archetypes** | 77 monsters: 5 to 7 in each area (11 in the Void Rift). The first comes with the area and the rest are unlocked in the Bestiary. Each has its own **HP, speed, spawn rate, pack size, gold**, **material** it drops, weaknesses and resistances, and an **archetype**: Slime, Beast, Undead, Demon, Elemental, Humanoid, Plant or Dragon. Wandering Woods: Green Slime (Green Slime), Twiggling (Twigs), Forest Wolf (Wolf Pelt + Beast Bone), Goblin (Scrap), Brown Bear (Bear Claw + Beast Bone). Fey Grove: Toadstool (Mushroom Cap), Pixie (Pixie Dust), Unicorn (Enchanted Bone + Unicorn Horn), Walking Man Trap (Enchanted Thorn), Treant (Sentient Sap), **Stone Golem** (Awoken Rock; rarer Obsidian and Iron Ore). Graveyard: Skeleton (Undead Bone), Zombie (Rotting Flesh, and sometimes gear), Vampire Bat (Vampire Essence + Bat Wing), Cultist (Sacred Text + Robe Piece), Wraith (Nightmare Wisp). Some monsters drop **two materials**: the second comes at half the drop chance. **Golems** (Constructs) drop several, each rarer than the last and rolling on its own; the Bestiary tags the rarer ones Uncommon, Rare, Very Rare or Legendary. Their metals and gems pay for Refine and Enchant. Besides the Stone, Ore and Geode Golems: the **Venomite Golem** (Venom Caverns: Cobalt Ore, rarer Venomite and Mithril Ore), the **Glacial Golem** (Frost Peaks: Frost Iron, rarer Coldsteel) and **Crystal Golem** (Frost Peaks: Aquamarine, rarer Sapphire), the **Sky Golem** (Ascendant Steps: Skysteel, rarer Orichalcum), the **Storm Golem** (Cloud Fortress: Opal, rarer Stormstone), the **Meteor Golem** (Meteor Field: Star Iron, rarer Adamantite) and the **Void Golem** (Void Rift: Voidsteel, rarer Void Prism). Crypt: Carrion Crow (Undead Feather), Possessed Puppet (Possessed String), Bone Knight (Enchanted Bone, and sometimes gear), Possessed Armor (Enchanted Scrap, and sometimes gear), Mummy (Possessed Cloth), Necromancer (Forbidden Text); the Puppet and the Armor are **Constructs**, a new archetype with its own evolution tree. Shadowy Depths: Gloomcrawler (Dark Claw), Duskmoth (Gloom Silk), Shadow Haunt (Shadow Wisp), Gazing Eye (Umbral Eyeball), Umbral Ooze (Umbral Slime), Abyssal Lurker (Umbral Pearl). Ember Mines: Imp (Demon Bone + Demon Wing), Magma Slime (Magma Slime), Fire Beetle (Chitin), Salamander (Ember), Hellhound (Demon Tooth + Demon Bone), **Ore Golem** (Iron Ore; rarer Silver Ore and Gold Ore), **Geode Golem** (Quartz; rarer Amethyst, Topaz, Emerald and Diamond). Venom Caverns: Kobold, Basilisk, Toxic Bloat, Broodmother, Cave Troll. Peaks: Ice Wolf, Frost Golem, Snow Wraith, Yeti, Frost Sprite. Ascendant Steps: Harpy, Snow Owl, Griffin, Ice Wyvern, Frost Giant. Cloud Fortress: Cloudling, Thunderbird, Sky Knight, Valkyrie, Storm Titan (drop Storm Feathers and Thunder Crystals). Meteor Field: Meteorite, Comet Wisp, Rock Mite, Astral Sentinel, Star Eater (drop Meteor Iron and Stardust). Rift: Shadow Slime, Void Horror, Lich, Shade, Watcher, Dark Knight, Succubus, Chimera, Void Wyrm, Behemoth. **Guardians** are boss versions of an area's monsters, met only in its Guardian Challenge, each dropping its own material (10 per kill): the **King of Slimes** (Royal Slime), **Pixie Queen** (Regal Essence), **Skeleton King** (Calcium Crystals), **Awoken Lich** (Eldritch Texts), **Beholden Watcher** (Everwatching Eyes) and **Demon Lord** (Forsaken Souls) for the first six areas; later areas still use one of their own monsters for now, and the Void Rift has the Time Eater. Guardian materials go into each area's prized recipes (e.g. the Slime Vial needs Royal Slime, the Flame Brand Forsaken Souls). New areas reuse the materials of the area before them. |
| **Bestiary** (gold) | Each area starts with one monster; **unlock** the others to grow its horde (a locked monster is a grey square, with a silver outline once you can afford it; gold means it can be Empowered). **Training** opens once you've slain 20 slimes (the Train button shows the count until then) and **Empower** once you've slain 100 slimes (a one-time popup explains the Bestiary). The Bestiary is laid out like the Hunters tab: each area's monsters (the current area first) as a **4-wide grid** of portraits with their level (or Unlock), a border glowing gold when you can afford to Empower (or unlock) one and unspent evolution points on the corner; selecting one pins its card to the bottom (tap it for the full view). Empower a monster with gold (the ×1/×10/×100/MAX switch in a monster's full view sets how many sessions): each session adds +4.2% HP, +20% gold, +2.1% material drops and +4.5% spawns (so the horde keeps up with your damage, and more monsters mean more gold); every 5 sessions is a level (and an evolution point), up to **Lv 30**, where its evolution tree is exactly complete (about ×7 HP, ×30 gold, ×7.5 spawns). The first sessions are cheap and each costs 12.5% more than the last, so a monster reaches Lv 30 about two areas later (Green Slimes around the end of the Restless Graveyard, or soon after reaching the Forbidden Crypt). Each card shows what the monster drops, its level and progress toward the next. Tap a monster (cards say **Expand ›**) for its full view, with two sub-tabs: **Stats** (what it drops, Empower, its stats including how many you've slain) and **Evolution** (its tree; the tab shows unspent points). Each row of the tree opens after slaying enough of that monster (100 for the root, 1,000 for the branches, 5,000 for the signature nodes, 20,000 for the capstone; rarer monsters need fewer), so you can't jump ahead. Every tree is laid out like the **Wandering Woods** monsters' trees (designed in the Skill Trees editor): a root, three branches (gold, packs or spawns, material drops), an upper row whose centre node needs all three branches, and a capstone that costs whatever points are left (29 in all, so every tree completes at Lv 30). The Woods monsters have trees of their own: the **Green Slime** (Sticky Coins, Slime Squad, Treasure Gel, Bursting Ooze and **Splitting Slime**: a slain one has a 25% chance per rank to split into two half-size slimes with half its health, which don't split again), the **Twiggling** (Kindling Harvest, Spreading Spores, Bountiful Bundle, Golden Sap and **Fire Starter**: one that dies Burning has a 10% chance per rank to leave burning sticks that hurt the monsters walking over them for 4s), the **Forest Wolf** (**Hunter Hunter**, 3 points: +50% gold and drops, and wolves lunge at Hunters who come within reach) and the **Goblin** (Stolen Wealth, Angry Horde, War Chest, Warbands and **Scavenged Goods**: +2.5% chance per rank of a piece of Wandering Woods loot). Every other monster copies its archetype's tree, named in the same spirit: Slimes (Gelatinous Bulk, Sticky Coins, Slime Squad, Ooze Trail, Treasure Gel, Wobbling Mass, Bursting Ooze, Slime Court), Plants (Deep Roots, Seed Harvest, Tangled Thicket, Spreading Spores, Bountiful Bloom, Thorny Growth, Golden Sap, World Tree), Beasts (Thick Hide, Hunting Grounds, Pack Hunters, Valuable Pelts, Prized Pelts, Feral Instinct, Alpha Stock, Apex Herd), Humanoids (Loot Sacks, Stolen Wealth, Angry Horde, Supply Lines, War Chest, Bounties, Warbands, Warlord), the Undead (Restless Dead, Grave Goods, Mass Grave, Shambling Horde, Tithe, Bone Harvest, Rising Horde, Endless Legion), Demons (Infernal Pact, Blood Money, Summoning Circle, Brimstone, Hoarded Souls, Bloodlust, Hellspawn, Hellgate), Elementals (Condensed Core, Crystal Heart, Elemental Surge, Shard Harvest, Charged Core, Unstable Essence, Shard Shedding, Primordial), Constructs (Reinforced Frame, Salvage Value, Assembly Line, Plating, Scrap Value, Spare Parts, Production Run, Animated Legion) and Dragons (Dragon Hoard, Gilded Lair, Clutch, Shed Scales, Golden Scales, Wyrmblood, Dragon's Bounty, Elder Wyrm). About 50 monsters swap a **signature node** of their own into the centre, worth the same 4 points, built on the Woods mechanics or two new ones: swarms **split** (Umbral Ooze's Splitting Ooze, Magma Slime's Molten Split, Cloudling's Cloudburst, Imp Swarm, Mite Swarm, Brood Hatch, Severed Strings), plants leave **burning debris** (Smoldering Caps, Burning Maw, Kindling Bark), predators **lunge** (Bear Charge, Blood Dive, Hellfire Pounce, Pack Leader, Avalanche Charge, Diving Talons, Stampede and more), hoarders drop their area's **loot** (Ritual Offerings, Grave Robbing, Shiny Hoard, Frozen Treasure, Spoils of War, Wyrm's Hoard and more), the undead **rise again** (Skeleton's Reassemble, Zombie's Undying, Oath Unbroken, Unwrapped, Restless Spirit, Phylactery: 15% chance per rank that a slain one gets back up once at half its HP) and elementals **burst** (Pixie Dust Burst, Gloom Burst, Bloated Burst, Frost Nova, Shatter, Overload, Impact, Starfall, Meteoric Core, Void Rupture, Supernova: 15% chance per rank that a slain one hurts every monster close by, though not Guardians, for half its max HP). **Every monster has its own tree** (evolving Green Slimes doesn't evolve Magma Slimes), and its capstone is its **Legacy** (named "A Green Slime's Legacy" and so on): besides its own bonus, every monster of its archetype, wherever it's hunted, gains a small chance to drop a material (a share of its drop chance). A monster whose boss version is its area's Guardian passes on the Guardian's material at 10% (Green Slime: Royal Slime to every slime; Pixie: Regal Essence; Skeleton: Calcium Crystal; Mummy: Eldritch Text; Gazing Eye: Everwatching Eye; Imp: Forsaken Soul); the others pass on their rarest drop at half its share (Geode Golem: Diamonds to every Construct), or their only drop at 25% (Twiggling: Twigs to every Plant). Ranks above a node's new maximum are given back as points. The Brown Bear's Legacy passes on Bear Claws. Legacies stack, and the monster's Stats show the **Legacy drops** its archetype has. Saves from before Lv 30, from before the trees took their own shapes, and from before they were rebuilt on the Woods trees refund their evolution points (the Woods monsters keep theirs). Tapping a node shows the **Points required** and **Points available** under its name and a big **Evolve** button. Permanent, and it applies wherever the monster is hunted, including by stationed Hunters. Older saves turn each Swarm and Bounty level into an Empower session. |
| **Hunters** (gold) | A Hunter's full view lists their **Abilities** in their own section of the Overview tab (after Training), in a framed panel, each on its own bordered tile with its name, an **Active** or **Passive** tag and what it does (Galladair's **Primal Hunter**: deal 50% bonus damage to Beast type monsters; Deku's **Spirit Wolves**, **Root Ripper** and **Pack Leader**; and so on), then the weapons they use. A newly recruited Guild Hunter comes with their own gear, about as good as yours: a new 1★ piece for each slot that fits them, from recipes whose materials you've found, as close as possible to (not above) the tier of what you wear in that kind of slot. Every Hunter, you included, **trains** with gold: each session adds a little damage and costs a bit more than the last. Enough sessions raise their **level** (3 sessions for Lv 2, then 4 more for Lv 3, and so on), and every level earns a **skill point** for their **skill tree**. Every Guild Hunter's tree is their own, built from the same small steps as yours (Attack Power +20%, Attack Speed +5%, crit +2.5%, cooldowns 5% per rank, and so on) but leaning into what they do: Galladair's pierces and splits his arrows (Piercing Arrows, Split Shot, and **Volley** for one more arrow), Glimmer's widens and hastens fireballs, Andrejz's spreads and lengthens his puddles, Deku's and Theon's make their summons fiercer and sooner, Alias's is gold and loot, Lance's is shields for everyone, Wilhelm's and Ted's are crits, Gideon's, Sciirj's, Themus's, Norm's and Thomas's grow and speed up their specials, Okrin's chills and shatters, and so on. A node needs a point in one of the nodes touching it in the row above, and its row must be open: rows lower down need a number of points spent in that tree first (a Guild Hunter's rows open at 5, 15 and 30; your third row at 5; a locked row shows a 2/5 bar across its top), set per tree in the Skill Trees editor. Some nodes cost more than one point (shown on the node), so every Guild Hunter's first tree costs exactly 39 points and is complete at **Lv 40**. Saves from before the trees were redesigned get every Guild Hunter's points back (both trees). Then a gold **Ascend** node appears (10 points, so **Lv 50**); Guild Hunters can't train past Lv 50 until they ascend. Their training cost grows 8.5% per session up to Lv 30, then 3% per session (a little faster than the damage each session adds), so gold paces the whole climb to Ascension. Ascending gives them a new title (e.g. Andrejz the Alchemist becomes the Archalchemist), keeps Lv 50, starts the level curve over (1 session to Lv 51, 2 to Lv 52, ...) at the same training price, opens an **Ascended tree** (50 points: Awakening, Mastery, Fervor and Resolve, three deep nodes in their speciality, Precision and Fortune, and a capstone of their own that opens at Lv 100) and raises the cap to **Lv 100**. **Your Hunter** ("the Hunter") has a five-wide first tree, laid out in the Skill Trees editor as two diamonds. The root (Hunter's Instinct) opens Attack Power, Attack Speed and Recovery Speed; their wings lead to the **Flame Burst** and **Frost Nova** tap abilities (with Tap Power, Eagle Eye, Piercing Shot and Tap Size between), then Wide Taps, Prosperity and Bulwark meet at **Apex Hunter** (ranged and magic attacks fire an extra projectile; melee attacks follow up with another strike, and dagger bursts throw one more dagger). Below it, Reach, **Quick Casting** (gear abilities and your tap ability recharge 10% faster per rank) and Long Shot lead out to Deadly Taps, Fortune, Treasure Sense and **Extended Effects** (the status effects you set off last 10% longer per rank), then **Thunderclap**, Hunter's Haste, **Brutal Criticals** (+50% crit damage) and **Melting Miasma**, and Golden Touch, Shield Wall, Bounty, Keen Eye and Hunter's Might come back together at **Legend** (7 points). The steps are small and steady (Attack Power +20%, Attack Speed +5%, Recovery Speed 5% per rank). Each node needs one of the nodes touching it in the row above, and rows open at 5, 10, 15, 20, 35 and 55 points spent; the whole tree takes 89 points. Each tap ability, equipped under **Tap Abilities** on your Overview, below your Abilities (the Overview shows the one equipped, or the plain **Blast** in your weapon's type, with a **Change** button listing those unlocked), makes a tap blast deal its element and always set off its effect on everything it hits: Flame Burst sets them burning (at most once every 3s, sooner with Quick Casting; between, its taps still deal Fire damage), Thunderclap arcs a bolt of lightning from each to another monster nearby, Frost Nova chills them for 3s, and Melting Miasma poisons them and, every 4s, leaves a poison puddle the size of the blast. Saves from before the latest rebuild get their first-tree points back (unlocked tap abilities stay unlocked). Below Legend is their own gold **Ascend** node (10 points) at **Lv 100**, which makes them **the Slayer**: the level curve starts over and the **Slayer's tree** (100 points, also five wide: Slayer's Oath, Carnage, Frenzy, Unbreakable, Far Sight, Plunder, Precision, Tap Fury, Tap Reach, Iron Skin, Spoils, and **Godslayer** at Lv 200) opens up, with training to **Lv 200**. Once a Hunter has ascended, a switch at the top of their Skills tab flips between the new tree and their first one. Their training cost grows 7.5% per session up to Lv 30, then 3% per session like the Guild Hunters', so each richer area pays for the next stretch of levels all the way to Lv 200. Lance's root, **Zone of Protection**, is his shield, and his **Rallying Oath** gives every other Hunter a hit. The **Hunter Guild** has 13 recruitable Hunters (a locked Hunter's card tells a short story of where they are and how to win them over), each with their own **attack style**, range and perk (table below). Each card shows the Hunter's art, a gold dot in the corner counting unspent skill points, their name, ability, location and DPS, equipped gear and a **Train** button (the ×1/×10/×100/MAX switch under Training in each Hunter's full view sets how many sessions, and the first-train popup explains it). **Tap a card** (they say **Expand ›** in the corner) for a full-screen view with three sub-tabs: **Overview** (training, **Abilities**, then DPS, damage, attack rate, range, stun time, shield, crit, **enemies slain**, stationing), **Equipment** (each slot, each piece on a card with its rarity-framed icon, rarity, stars, damage types and stats, then its modifiers (Refines on grey tiles, Enchants on violet ones; a "..." tile when more than two rows' worth), tap one to choose from your unequipped gear that fits), **Skills** (the tree; tapping a node shows the **Points required** and **Points available** under its name and a big **Acquire** button) (your Hunter's tap abilities sit on their Overview, under Tap Abilities, below their Abilities); close it with the floating **✕** at the bottom (on the right, or the left when left-handed) to return to the battlefield. The location under their name is a button that opens the all-areas Hunter view. **Station** up to 3 Hunters per area (besides yours); Hunters sharing an area farm it together. They earn gold and materials there at 80% efficiency (offline too). Visit their area and they fight beside you. Monsters head for the **nearest** Hunter, so stationed Hunters can be stunned too. Each area brings something new: the Wandering Woods and Fey Grove are the baseline, the Restless Graveyard adds **flying** monsters (they pass over rocks, trees and graves instead of walking around them), and the Forbidden Crypt adds **ranged** monsters: the Necromancer (and, in the Shadowy Depths, the Shadow Haunt and the long-range Gazing Eye) stops at range and hurls bolts at the nearest Hunter (it never reaches them, so never runs off with its loot). A bolt that reaches a Hunter is dodged by light armor, blocked by a shield charge, or stuns them, unless they stop it first: a Hunter with a **melee weapon** breaks any bolt that comes within reach (spending their next attack), and one wearing a **shield** deflects bolts that reach them, though their attacks pause for 1s. Gideon's Bone Wall stops bolts too. The Bestiary tags monsters **Flying** or **Ranged**. **Clarity Border** (Settings → Display): a white outline round every Hunter, monster and monster projectile so they stand out from the ground and scenery: Off, Low (a faint edge, the default), Moderate or Strong. |

A test keeps the steps in place. Across all of it, Ranged, Melee and **Magic** weapons (wands, staffs and focuses, for Andrejz and Glimmer), Armor and Accessories modify damage, attack rate, range, crit, stun time, shield charges, gold, drops, area size and pierce. Every weapon has a **damage type**: Physical, Fire, Acid, Frost, Radiant, Poison, Arcane, Decay, **Lightning** or Void (most bows, crossbows and spears are Physical). A Hunter deals their weapon's type, or Physical without one. Andrejz's potions (Poison) and Glimmer's fireballs (Fire) always deal their own type. **Weapons with several damage types** work one of two ways: most **split each hit evenly** between their types (a Fire and Frost sword hitting for 100 deals 50 Fire and 50 Frost), each part checked against the monster's weaknesses and resistances on its own and rolling its own status effect (against a monster weak to Fire and resisting Frost: 50 × 1.5 + 50 × 0.5); projectile weapons can instead fire **one type per projectile**, taking turns (a Frost and Lightning repeater's bolts are each Frost or Lightning at full damage). **Special** is the damage type shown for weapons that change elements or deal too many to list (a rainbow tag; the Filter can narrow by it): the first is **Elemental Cataclysm**, an Exalted repeater from the Void Rift (Soul Gems, Void Dust, Shadow Gel) whose bolts start with Arcane and cycle through every element (Fire, Frost, Acid, Poison, Lightning, Radiant, Decay, Void), each bolt in its element's colour with a 25% chance to trigger that element's effect. Each part gets the Hunter's bonuses to its own type (gear can carry a **type bonus**, e.g. +20% Fire damage). **Every damage bonus multiplies**: a bonus against an archetype (Galladair's +50% vs Beasts, rising to +100% and +150% through her skill trees, a Fang Talisman's +5%) × the monster's weakness or resistance to the type × any bonus to that type. Galladair with a Fire bow (2 damage) against a Fire-weak Beast with a ×1.5 Beast bonus: 2 × 1.5 (weak) = 3, × 1.5 (vs Beasts) = 4.5. Types show as coloured tags on weapons, in the Hunter view and in the damage numbers, and the Filter can narrow by them; every monster has **weaknesses** (×1.5 damage from those types) and **resistances** (×0.5), shown in the Bestiary and the area details; weak hits show bigger damage numbers with a “!”. Hits can also trigger their type's **status effect**, at the weapon's own chance (shown on its card; unarmed Hunters never trigger one, and among Physical weapons only blades can): **Fire** burns (30% of the hit again over 2s, with a small chance each tick to spread to monsters right next to it, weaker each time), **Poison** poisons (40% of the hit over 4s, plus 1–6% of the monster's max HP by weapon rarity, Common to Exalted; a tenth of that on Guardians), **Frost** chills (half speed for 1.2s), **Acid** drops an acid puddle that hurts everything in it for 3s, **Radiant** bursts for 60% of the hit on everything nearby, **Decay** gives the monster a dark aura that hurts the monsters around it for 4s, **Arcane** strips its resistances for 4s, **Lightning** arcs: 60% of the hit jumps to another creature within reach of the one struck (any one, at random), and strikes every creature the bolt passes through on the way (the Skyward Staff, Stormbreaker, Thunder Repeater and Tome of Storms deal Lightning), and **Physical** (daggers, swords and spears) makes it **bleed** (25% of the hit over 3s, dripping crimson pixels). **A monster that resists a damage type is immune to its status effect** (no burning for fire-resistant monsters, no chill for frost-resistant ones, and so on), until Arcane strips its resistances. Bleeding is the only effect that stacks, up to 8 bleeds at once; every other effect is one instance per monster, and a new one refreshes it (keeping the stronger; a monster has at most one acid puddle). **Void** opens a **gravity well** on the monster it hits: for 1.5s, monsters within reach are pulled in toward it until they touch it, bunching them up for area attacks (Guardians and Void-resistant monsters hold their ground). Glimmer's fireballs always burn; Andrejz's puddle ticks don't re-poison. Effects show on monsters as flames, bubbles, a frosty ring, a dark aura, violet sparks or a magenta spiral (a gravity well). ## Sound

Short retro clips in `public/sfx/`, played through Web Audio by `src/ui/sound.ts` once the player first touches the screen (browsers and phone web views block audio before that). **Settings → Audio** (the first card in Settings) has an On/Off switch and a volume slider for all audio, and under it the same for music and for sound effects (switching one off keeps its slider where it was). The volumes multiply: a sound effect plays at All audio × Sound effects. What plays:

- **Buttons**: a click; switching tabs or sub-tabs, a softer navigate blip. A button with its own sound plays only that one.
- **Getting stronger**: a power-up for Train, Empower, a skill or evolution node, and crafting. A level up, a Hunter recruited, a monster or area unlocked, or an event won plays a fanfare instead.
- **Battlefield**: a pop for each monster slain and a big explosion for a Guardian; a zap for your tap; an explosion for fireballs and other blasts; a knock when a shield blocks; a hurt sound when your Hunter (or, softer, a Guild Hunter) is stunned; a coin when gear drops; a defeat jingle when a Guardian gets away.

Which clip plays for each of the 33 moments (and its volume, minimum gap, pitch spread, pitch shift in semitones and length) lives in `src/ui/sfxConfig.ts`, edited in the **Pocket Hunter Sound Board** (an Artifact: https://claude.ai/artifact/VMAwWuVkaSW8cH5DN6cgBW). To sync, its `events` collection is copied into `SFX`, and any uploaded clips (its `clips` collection, base64 MP3/WAV/OGG) are written to `public/sfx/<name>.mp3` and referenced by name. Besides the interface, progress, event and battlefield moments, every attack makes a sound (a **melee swing** for swords, glaives, spears, hammers and daggers; a **ranged shot** for bows, crossbows, guns and the unarmed bolt) and magic sounds like its element: one moment per damage type (fire, frost, lightning, poison, acid, radiant, arcane, decay, void, and earth for physical), played for spells, spell blasts, elemental tap abilities and lightning from the sky (the renderer reads the element from the effect's colour). Volumes are levelled by loudness, measured on each clip as shaped: the louder of the standard measure (EBU R128, loudest 400 ms) and the loudest 50 ms less 5 dB, since the 400 ms window badly underrates short clicks and hits. Big moments sit at -14 LUFS, the tab and stuns -17 to -19, the constant combat sounds -20, and the two heard most, a button press (every X included) -21 and a monster slain -23. Most clips come from the free **RPG Essentials** SFX pack (`public/sfx/rpg-*.mp3`, silence trimmed and peak-levelled). Pitch and length are independent: `src/ui/stretch.ts` stretches a clip without changing its pitch (WSOLA: overlapping 30 ms slices, each nudged up to 8 ms to line up with the last), and a pitch shift plays it faster or slower after stretching by that rate, so it ends at the length asked for. The game stretches each moment's clip once, when it loads.

Kills and taps can fire many times a second, so each sound has a minimum gap between plays, a little random pitch, and at most 8 play at once. Sound pauses while the app is in the background.

## Tech

- **TypeScript + HTML5 Canvas**, bundled with **Vite**. No game engine and no image assets: everything is drawn with simple shapes.
- **Look**: Game Boy Advance-style. A bundled pixel font (Pixelify Sans, so it works offline), square windows with thick borders and hard shadows, and a battlefield drawn at one canvas pixel per screen point, then scaled up crisply. Each area tints the menus with a muted (desaturated) version of its palette over a darker backdrop, so the brightly coloured battlefield stands out, and gives the battlefield its own ground colour (`palette` and `ground` in `AREAS`; applied by `src/ui/theme.ts`).
- **Capacitor** wraps the web build as a native Android app (`android/`). The same code becomes the iOS app later.
- **Snappy menus in big saves**: menus only build what's on screen. The Equipment list draws 48 tiles at a time (more as you scroll), the Bestiary draws its portraits a batch per frame, Inventory builds only its open sub-tab, and off-screen cards skip layout (`content-visibility`). Refreshes rewrite text only when it changed, farming rates and DPS shown on cards are recomputed at most once a second (or right after a tap), monster portraits are measured together then drawn, and gear lookups by id use an index instead of scanning the inventory.
- Saves use `@capacitor/preferences` (SharedPreferences / UserDefaults natively, localStorage on web). It autosaves every 10s and when the app is backgrounded.

```
src/
  core/        pure game logic, no DOM (unit-tested)
    balance.ts   every tunable number and content table (areas, monsters, archetypes, Hunters, items, hangar)
    game.ts      economy & progression: areas, events & Guardians, Hunters & stationing, bestiary, shops, forge, background farming
    field.ts     the battlefield simulation: spawning, movement, attack styles, ranges, stuns & shields, fleeing, Guardians
    sampler.ts   background battlefield samples: a hidden field measures each farming group, for offline and stationed rates
    offline.ts   offline cap + ticket regen
    state.ts     save format + migration
    save.ts      persistence
  render/      canvas: battlefield view, particles, sprite loader
  assets/sprites/  drop-in artwork (optional)
  ui/          DOM panels (Hunters / Areas / Bestiary / Equipment / Events), modals
tests/         vitest: economy, field, events, saves, and a bot that plays the real battlefield to check pacing
```

## Running it

```bash
npm install
npm run dev        # play in a browser at http://localhost:5173 (use phone view in devtools)
npm test           # unit tests + progression pacing check
```

Balancing: `SIM_SWEEP=1 npx vitest run tests/progression.test.ts --silent=false` runs a bot on the real (headless) battlefield and prints when each area unlocks, both for a **typical player** and for nonstop play. The typical player has a 40-minute first session, then checks in at 08:00 (15 min), 13:00 (10 min) and 18:00 (20 min) daily, with offline gains in between. Current pacing for that player: **Fey Grove ~day 0.6**, **Graveyard ~day 2.6**, **Crypt ~day 5.6**, **Depths ~day 10.5**, **Caves ~day 18**, **Venom Caverns ~day 23.5**, **Frost Peaks ~day 32**, **Ascendant Steps ~day 41.5**, **Cloud Fortress ~day 48.5**, **Meteor Field ~day 58.5**, **Void Rift ~day 72**, and the **Time Eater** around **day 90** (the Rift and Time Eater estimated from kill rates). Every Guardian through the Meteor Field falls in one to three tries, so each area's mastery is the clock. Each area's monsters reach Lv 30 about two areas later (Green Slimes during the Crypt). Gold runs about 1/s in the Woods, 100/s in the Grove, 100K/s in the Graveyard, 10M/s in the Crypt, 100B/s in the Depths, 100T/s in the Mines and Caverns, and sextillions per second in the Rift. `SIM_OUT=pace.json` writes the typical player's unlock times in hours. The pacing test (run by CI after the APK is built, without blocking it) flags drift out of range. `CALIBRATE=1 npx vitest run tests/calibrate.test.ts --silent=false` compares the offline/background model against the live battlefield for several Hunter strengths. The gates are each area's Guardian HP and mastery kills (`AREAS` in `balance.ts`).
